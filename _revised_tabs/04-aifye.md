## The AI-FYE

> The AI-FYE links two courses so first-year students can practice using generative AI while also studying why it matters.

The basic idea is simple: do not treat generative AI as an add-on that appears after college habits are already formed. Put it in the first year, make the use visible, and teach students how to work with it appropriately from the beginning.

The AI-FYE combines **FYS 111: The AI Experience** and **COM 216: AI and the Human Future**. The two courses are connected, but they do different jobs.

### FYS 111: The AI Experience

FYS 111 focuses on the habits and systems students need in college with generative AI in the room. You practice prompting, saving conversations, comparing models, building useful workflows, organizing coursework, and noticing when a tool is helping or getting in the way.

The course also looks at responsible use. Generative AI can be useful, persuasive, shallow, surprising, and wrong. Students need practice seeing those differences before the stakes get high.

### COM 216: AI and the Human Future

COM 216 is the larger four-credit course. It studies human communication from oral culture through writing, print, electronic media, the internet, and generative AI. It asks what happens when technologies change how people read, write, remember, organize information, and make meaning.

### Why link them?

The link gives the semester a rhythm. In FYS 111, you practice working with the tools and building habits. In COM 216, you use those habits to read, discuss, and write about larger questions. One course is not merely the support act for the other. Together, they create a first-year experience organized around the same problem from two directions: how to use generative AI well, and how to understand what it is changing.

By the end of the semester, you should have more than a grade. You should have a record of how your thinking with generative AI changed from the beginning of the course to the end.
