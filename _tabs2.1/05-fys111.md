## FYS 111: The AI Experience

> FYS 111 is a first-year course about learning college with generative AI in the room.

This course has its own subject: how students build useful, responsible, everyday practices with generative AI. It is connected to COM 216, but it is not simply a lab for COM 216. FYS 111 is where we slow down the habits students often develop by accident and turn them into things we can name, practice, and improve.

### What you do in FYS 111

**Build systems for college.** You use generative AI to help organize syllabi, deadlines, notes, study plans, and coursework. The point is not a perfect productivity system. The point is to build something that actually helps you stay oriented — and to understand how generative AI can be part of that, not just adjacent to it.

**Learn to work with a model, not just ask it questions.** The goal is to put generative AI into the loop of your thinking — starting tasks by asking how it can be part of them, pushing back when it goes wrong, redirecting when it drifts. A better conversation asks, redirects, compares, checks, and revises across several turns. You practice that. The judgment stays yours.

**Save the trail.** You learn to keep transcripts of your work. A transcript shows what you asked, what the model did, where it went wrong, and how you responded. That record is useful because it lets you see your own thinking in motion — and because in COM 216, the transcript is the work.

**Understand where it goes wrong.** People talk to generative AI like a someone. They feel met by it — and that is not simply a mistake, because the thing genuinely answers. A not-person that we relate to as a person: we have never had that before, and we do not yet have good instincts for it. FYS 111 looks holistically at what happens when humans develop relationships with these systems — not to frighten you or freak you out, but because responsible use requires knowing the risks, not just the capabilities.

### What you should leave with

By the end of FYS 111, you should have a working set of habits for using generative AI in college: how to start, how to check, how to revise, how to document, and how to decide whether the tool is actually helping. Those habits should travel well beyond this course.
