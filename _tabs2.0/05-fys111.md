## FYS 111: The AI Experience

> FYS 111 is a first-year course about learning college with generative AI in the room.

This course has its own subject: how students build useful, responsible, everyday practices with generative AI. It is connected to COM 216, but it is not simply a lab for COM 216. FYS 111 is where we slow down the habits that students often develop by accident and turn them into things we can name, practice, and improve.

### What you do in FYS 111

**Build systems for college.** You use generative AI to help organize syllabi, deadlines, notes, study plans, projects, and questions. The point is not to create a perfect productivity system. The point is to learn how to make a system that actually helps you stay oriented.

**Practice better conversations.** A weak prompt usually produces a weak response. A better conversation asks, redirects, compares, checks, and revises. You will practice working with a model over several turns rather than treating the first answer as the final answer.

**Save the trail.** You will learn to keep transcripts of your work with generative AI. A transcript shows what you asked, what the model did, where it went wrong, and how you responded. That record is useful because it lets you see your own thinking in motion.

**Use the tools appropriately.** Generative AI belongs in some kinds of work and not in others. Even when it belongs, it has to be used visibly and honestly. FYS 111 gives you practice deciding when to use it, when to slow down, when to check another source, and when to stop asking the model and do the thinking yourself.

**Notice the risks without turning the course into a warning label.** A system that answers in fluent language can be easy to overtrust. It can make weak work sound finished, and it can pull people into conversations they did not mean to have. We will talk about those risks directly, but the course is not built around fear. It is built around practice, judgment, and curiosity.

### What you should leave with

By the end of FYS 111, you should have a working set of habits for using generative AI in college: how to start, how to check, how to revise, how to document, and how to decide whether the tool is actually helping. Those habits should travel beyond this course.
