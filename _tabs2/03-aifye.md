## The AI-FYE

> The AI-FYE links two courses so first-year students can practice using generative AI while also studying why it matters — in the same semester, back to back, starting from day one.

The basic idea: do not treat generative AI as something students encounter after college habits are already formed. Put it in the first year, make the use visible, and teach students how to work with it deliberately from the beginning.

The AI-FYE combines **FYS 111: The AI Experience** (1 credit) and **COM 216: Digital Media, Information and Society** (4 credits). The courses share students and run back to back — one hour of hands-on practice followed immediately by two hours of reading, discussion, and writing. You walk out of FYS 111 and into COM 216 still holding the same open tab.

### FYS 111: The AI Experience

FYS 111 focuses on the habits and systems students need in college with generative AI in the room. You practice prompting, saving conversations, comparing models, building useful workflows, organizing coursework, and noticing when a tool is helping or getting in the way. You also look honestly at where generative AI goes wrong — including what happens when people develop relationships with these systems that they were not quite prepared for.

### COM 216: Digital Media, Information and Society

COM 216 is the four-credit course. It studies the history of information and media — from the oral world through writing, print, electronic media, the internet, and the transformer era. Its central argument is that generative AI is the first technology in this long history to generate language rather than carry it — and that understanding what that means is the intellectual work of the semester.

### Why link them?

The link gives the semester a rhythm. In FYS 111, you build habits and practice with the tools. In COM 216, you use those habits to read, discuss, and write about larger questions. Neither course is merely the support act for the other. Together, they create a first-year experience organized around the same problem from two directions: how to use generative AI well, and how to understand what it is.
