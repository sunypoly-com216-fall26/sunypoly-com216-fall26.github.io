## An alien species

> Generative AI is arriving like an alien species. We did not decide whether it would appear in college, work, search, writing, art, coding, advising, entertainment, and everyday conversation. It is here, and the interesting question is how to study it well.

A short video introducing this metaphor will go here: [video link to add].

The metaphor is a doorway into the course. It is not a claim that the technology is magic. It is a way to slow down our first reaction, because most familiar labels do not quite work.

When students first use generative AI, they often reach for comparisons they already know. It seems like a search engine, because you can ask it questions. It seems like a calculator, because it gives answers quickly. It seems like a database, because it can produce information on demand. It seems like earlier media, because it works through language, screens, networks, and software.

Those comparisons help for about five minutes. Then they break.

Generative AI does not simply retrieve a page, perform a fixed operation, or play back a stored answer. It generates fresh language in response to context. It can summarize, imitate, transform, draft, compare, invent, explain, and revise. It can also drift, bluff, flatten differences, miss what matters, and make weak thinking look finished.

That is why this course treats generative AI as something students need to study on its own terms. If you treat it as a search box, you will mostly get search-box results. If you treat it as an answer machine, you will learn to collect answers instead of doing college-level thinking. If you treat it as an alien species, you start from curiosity: What kind of thing is this? What does it do to language? What does it do to attention, memory, writing, judgment, and work?

That is the tone of the course. Curious first. Useful next. Critical all the way through.
