<h2>The transcript is the work</h2>

<p>Generative AI can produce an answer. This course is more interested in the path that produced it.</p>

<div class="collapsible-section">
  <div class="section-toggle" onclick="this.parentElement.classList.toggle('open')">Why the conversation matters</div>
  <p class="section-lead">A lot of weak AI use disappears into the final product.</p>
  <div class="section-body">
    <p>A student asks for help, accepts too much, changes a few words, and turns in something that looks smoother than the thinking behind it. That is not the practice this course is trying to build.</p>
    <p>Here, the conversation matters. The transcript shows the questions you asked, the assumptions you started with, the moments when the model helped, the moments when it drifted, and the places where you pushed back. It shows whether you treated the model as an answer machine or as something to think with carefully.</p>
    <p>That is the difference between using generative AI to avoid learning and using generative AI to make learning visible.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible-section">
  <div class="section-toggle" onclick="this.parentElement.classList.toggle('open')">What a good transcript shows</div>
  <p class="section-lead">A useful transcript does not have to be perfect. It should show movement.</p>
  <div class="section-body">
    <p>You ask a question, get a response, notice something, redirect, compare, challenge, narrow, expand, or revise. Sometimes the most useful moment is the one where the model gets something wrong and you catch it.</p>
    <p>The transcript is not valuable because the model is impressive. It is valuable because it gives us a record of your judgment.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible-section">
  <div class="section-toggle" onclick="this.parentElement.classList.toggle('open')">Questions worth keeping</div>
  <p class="section-lead">These are not rhetorical. They are the actual questions the course is organized around.</p>
  <div class="section-body">
    <ul>
      <li>Can generative AI help me read something difficult without doing the reading for me?</li>
      <li>When does working with it make me sharper — and when does it make me lazy?</li>
      <li>How do I know when it is wrong?</li>
      <li>What is mine in this document, and how would I show that to someone who asked?</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible-section">
  <div class="section-toggle" onclick="this.parentElement.classList.toggle('open')">The AI Learner's Permit</div>
  <p class="section-lead">On the first day of class, every student is issued an <strong>AI Learner's Permit</strong>.</p>
  <div class="section-body">
    <p>The permit gives you permission to use AI and large language models in COM 216 and to submit assignments that incorporate AI — provided the work is documented, visible, and yours.</p>
    <p>Earning the permit requires completing a series of exercises that demonstrate critical AI use: saving your conversations, building a workspace that makes your thinking visible, and developing habits that keep the judgment yours. The permit is not a trophy and not a loophole. It is a record that you know how to work with these systems accountably.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible-section">
  <div class="section-toggle" onclick="this.parentElement.classList.toggle('open')">Beyond the permit: Google Professional Certificates</div>
  <p class="section-lead">Students also have the opportunity to complete two Google Professional Certificates — <strong>AI Essentials</strong> and <strong>AI Professional</strong>.</p>
  <div class="section-body">
    <p>These come alongside companion exercises that offer a critical perspective on the training. The certificates are recognized by employers. The companion exercises are where the course's analytical work meets the practical skills the certificates build.</p>
    <p>The goal is not to learn to use AI. The goal is to learn how not to be used by it.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible-section">
  <div class="section-toggle" onclick="this.parentElement.classList.toggle('open')">A question worth asking</div>
  <p class="section-lead">One question we put directly to a model early in the course.</p>
  <div class="section-body">
    <pre>Some people say generative AI is arriving like an alien species. Is that a useful metaphor or a bad one? Why? What kind of thing are you when you generate language?</pre>
    <p>The point is not to let the model define the course. The point is to make the question visible, test the answer, and decide what we think after the conversation — including whether the model's answer tells us something about the question itself.</p>
  </div>
</div>
