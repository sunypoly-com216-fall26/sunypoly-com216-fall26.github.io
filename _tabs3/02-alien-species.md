<h2>An alien species of exotic mind-like entities</h2>

<p>Generative AI is arriving like an alien species of exotic mind-like entities. We did not decide whether it would appear in college, work, search, writing, art, coding, advising, entertainment, and everyday conversation. It is here — and the interesting question is how to study it honestly.</p>

<div class="collapsible-section">
  <div class="section-toggle" onclick="this.parentElement.classList.toggle('open')">Why the familiar comparisons break down</div>
  <p class="section-lead">The metaphor is a doorway into the course.</p>
  <div class="section-body">
    <p>It is not a claim that the technology is magic, or dangerous, or alive. It is a way to slow down our first reaction — because most of the familiar labels do not quite work.</p>
    <p>When students first use generative AI, they reach for comparisons they already know. It seems like a search engine, because you can ask it questions. It seems like a calculator, because it produces answers quickly. It seems like a database, because it can retrieve information on demand. Those comparisons help for about five minutes. Then they break.</p>
    <p>Generative AI does not retrieve a stored page, perform a fixed operation, or play back a recorded answer. It generates fresh language in response to context. It can summarize, imitate, transform, draft, compare, invent, explain, and revise. It can also drift, bluff, flatten differences, miss what matters, and make weak thinking look polished.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible-section">
  <div class="section-toggle" onclick="this.parentElement.classList.toggle('open')">What makes it genuinely strange</div>
  <p class="section-lead">People talk to it like a someone.</p>
  <div class="section-body">
    <p>They feel met by it — and that is not simply a mistake, because the thing genuinely answers. A not-person that we relate to as a person: we have never had that before, and we do not yet have good instincts for navigating it. Whether these systems understand anything — in any meaningful sense — is not a settled question. Serious people are working on it. This course takes the question seriously rather than settling it prematurely in either direction.</p>
    <p>That is why the alien species metaphor is useful. It asks you to start from curiosity rather than from a category you already know. What kind of thing is this? What does it do to language? What does it do to attention, memory, writing, and judgment?</p>
    <p>That is the tone of the course. Curious first. Useful next. Critical all the way through.</p>
  </div>
</div>
